FOREX BASICS

What Is a Spread in Forex?

By Ali Moayyed and Nasrin Kheyrdost

Learn what the forex spread means, how bid and ask prices work, how to calculate a spread in pips, and why it matters when understanding the cost of a trade.

When you look at a forex trading platform, you may notice that the displayed buying price and selling price are slightly different. That difference is called the spread. It is one of the basic costs traders need to understand before evaluating a trade.

For example, a platform might show EUR/USD with a bid price of 1.1000 and an ask price of 1.1002. The difference is 0.0002, equivalent to 2 pips under the usual pip convention for EUR/USD. This is an illustrative example, not a live market quote.

Key idea

Spread = Ask price − Bid price. The spread is the difference between the price at which a provider is willing to buy and the price at which it is willing to sell a currency pair at that moment.

1. What Is a Forex Spread?

A forex spread is the difference between the bid and ask prices quoted for a currency pair. The bid is generally the price at which you can sell the base currency, while the ask is generally the price at which you can buy it, subject to the product and execution conditions offered by your provider.

The two prices are often displayed together. Because the ask is usually higher than the bid, a trader entering and immediately closing a position under unchanged prices would generally face the spread as an initial trading cost, before commissions, slippage or other charges.

Spreads can change over time. They may differ between currency pairs, providers, account types and market conditions. A spread observed at one moment should not be assumed to remain the same.

2. What Are Bid and Ask Prices?

To understand the spread, first learn the meaning of the two prices that make it up.

Term Meaning Typical action
Bid The quoted price at which the provider is willing to buy. A trader generally sells at the bid.
Ask The quoted price at which the provider is willing to sell. A trader generally buys at the ask.
Spread The difference between the ask and the bid. It contributes to the cost of entering a trade.

Example: Reading a EUR/USD quote

Suppose a platform displays these illustrative prices:

  • Bid: 1.1000
  • Ask: 1.1002

A trader selling EUR/USD would generally transact at the bid, while a trader buying EUR/USD would generally transact at the ask. The difference between the two prices is 0.0002.

Actual execution can differ from a displayed quote. Prices may change before an order is filled, and the product's rules, available liquidity and order type can affect execution.

3. How to Calculate a Forex Spread

The basic calculation is simple: subtract the bid price from the ask price. To express the result in pips, compare the difference with the pip size conventionally used for that currency pair.

Formula

Spread in price terms = Ask − Bid

For many non-JPY currency pairs, one pip is 0.0001. For many JPY pairs, one pip is 0.01. Confirm the convention used for the instrument you are studying.

Example A: EUR/USD has a 2-pip spread

Assume the bid is 1.1000 and the ask is 1.1002.

1.1002 − 1.1000 = 0.0002

With a pip size of 0.0001:

0.0002 ÷ 0.0001 = 2 pips

The spread is 2 pips in this example. It does not, by itself, tell you the total cash cost of a position; position size, account currency and any additional charges also matter.

Example B: USD/JPY has a 3-pip spread

Suppose the bid is 150.00 and the ask is 150.03.

150.03 − 150.00 = 0.03

Using a conventional pip size of 0.01 for this example:

0.03 ÷ 0.01 = 3 pips

The spread is 3 pips. Some platforms also display fractional pip digits, so pay attention to the quote format.

4. How Does the Spread Affect Trading Costs?

The spread is one component of trading cost. When a position is opened, the bid and ask difference generally means that the position begins with an adverse price difference compared with immediately reversing the transaction at unchanged quotes.

The financial effect depends on the instrument, position size, pip value, account currency and execution. A spread of 2 pips does not represent the same cash amount for every trade.

Illustrative cost example

Imagine that the spread is 2 pips and that, for a particular position and account, one pip is worth $1. The spread-related amount would be approximately:

2 pips × $1 per pip = $2

This example assumes a known pip value and excludes commissions, slippage, financing and other charges. The actual value of a pip varies with the instrument, position size, exchange rate and account currency.

5. Fixed vs. Variable Spreads

Trading providers may offer different spread arrangements. The labels below describe common approaches, but the exact terms depend on the provider and account agreement.

Fixed spreads

A fixed spread is designed to remain at a stated level or within defined terms under specified conditions. However, a provider's terms may allow spreads to change during exceptional market conditions, and execution prices are not guaranteed.

Variable or floating spreads

A variable spread changes with market conditions. It may narrow when liquidity is strong and widen when liquidity is lower or volatility rises. The actual behaviour depends on the provider, instrument and trading environment.

Feature Fixed spread Variable spread
Typical behaviour Designed to stay near a stated level under defined terms. Changes as quotes and market conditions change.
Predictability May be more predictable in ordinary conditions. Can be narrow at some times and wider at others.
Important limitation Terms and exceptions still need to be checked. Displayed spreads can widen, especially during volatile periods.

Neither type is automatically cheaper in every situation. Compare the actual spread, commission, execution quality and other charges for the account and instrument you intend to study.

6. Why Do Spreads Change?

A spread is influenced by several factors. The same currency pair may have different spreads at different times or across different providers.

  • Liquidity: when fewer buyers and sellers are available at competitive prices, spreads may widen.
  • Volatility: rapid price changes can increase uncertainty and lead to wider quotes.
  • Economic announcements: major data releases and central-bank decisions can affect prices and liquidity.
  • Time of day: market activity and liquidity vary across trading sessions and around session transitions.
  • Currency pair: spreads differ across instruments; a widely traded pair is not guaranteed to have the lowest spread at every moment.
  • Provider and account type: pricing models, commissions and account terms can produce different total costs.
Beginner tip

Do not judge a provider by a single advertised minimum spread. Check the typical spread, when it was measured, any commission, and the terms that apply during volatile market conditions.

7. What Is the Difference Between a Spread and a Pip?

A pip is a unit commonly used to describe a change in a forex price. A spread is the difference between the ask and bid prices. A spread can be expressed in pips, but the two terms do not mean the same thing.

Concept What it describes Example
Pip A conventional unit for measuring price movement. EUR/USD moves from 1.1000 to 1.1005: 5 pips.
Spread The difference between ask and bid prices. Bid 1.1000, ask 1.1002: 2 pips.

Learn the underlying measurement first in What Is a Pip in Forex? Then return to this guide to understand how the bid-ask difference is expressed in pips.

8. Common Beginner Mistakes

  • Confusing spread with commission: the spread is the bid-ask difference; a commission is a separate charge that may apply to a trade.
  • Assuming a spread never changes: variable spreads can move as market conditions change.
  • Looking only at the advertised minimum: minimum values may not reflect the spread available when you trade.
  • Assuming a pip has the same cash value in every trade: pip value depends on the instrument, position size and account currency.
  • Ignoring other costs: commissions, slippage, overnight financing and other charges may affect the total.
  • Assuming a low spread makes a trade profitable: price movement can still result in a loss, and trading costs do not determine market direction.

9. Test Your Understanding

Try each question before opening the answer. These exercises test your understanding of spreads, not your ability to predict market movements.

Question 1

EUR/USD has a bid of 1.1050 and an ask of 1.1053. What is the spread in pips?

Show answer

The price difference is 1.1053 − 1.1050 = 0.0003. Using a pip size of 0.0001, the spread is 3 pips.

Question 2

Is a pip the same thing as a spread?

Show answer

No. A pip is a unit for describing price movement. The spread is the difference between the ask and bid prices, and it can be expressed in pips.

Question 3

Why might a variable spread widen around a major economic announcement?

Show answer

Volatility and uncertainty may rise while available liquidity changes. The result can be a wider difference between bid and ask prices, although the exact response depends on the market and provider.

Question 4

If a spread is 2 pips, can you calculate the exact cash cost without knowing anything else about the trade?

Show answer

No. You need information such as position size, pip value and account currency. Commissions, slippage and other costs may also apply.

10. Frequently Asked Questions

What is a spread in forex in simple terms?

It is the difference between the bid price and the ask price quoted for a currency pair.

How do I calculate the spread in pips?

Subtract the bid from the ask, then divide the price difference by the conventional pip size for that pair. Many non-JPY pairs use 0.0001 per pip, while many JPY pairs use 0.01.

Is a lower spread always better?

A lower spread can reduce one component of trading costs, but it does not automatically mean the overall service is cheaper. Check commissions, execution quality, slippage and other terms.

Why does the spread sometimes become wider?

Spreads may widen when liquidity is lower or volatility and uncertainty rise, such as around some economic announcements or market transitions.

Are spreads the only cost of forex trading?

No. Depending on the product and provider, other costs may include commissions, slippage, overnight financing and currency conversion charges.

Can a spread predict whether a trade will be profitable?

No. A spread is a pricing difference, not a directional signal. It does not predict future prices or guarantee a trading result.
