FOREX BASICS

What Is a Currency Pair in Forex?

By Ali Moayyed and Nasrin Kheyrdost

Learn how to read forex currency pairs, understand base and quote currencies, interpret exchange rates, and recognise what buying or selling a pair means.

Imagine you want to exchange euros for US dollars before a trip. The amount of dollars you receive depends on the exchange rate. Forex trading uses the same basic idea: one currency is valued against another.

In the forex market, currencies are normally quoted in pairs, such as EUR/USD, GBP/USD, or USD/JPY. The first currency is the one being measured; the second tells you the price in which that first currency is expressed.

Key idea

EUR/USD at 1.1000 means that one euro is quoted at 1.10 US dollars. It does not mean that one dollar is worth 1.10 euros.

1. What Is a Currency Pair?

A currency pair is a quotation that compares the value of one currency with the value of another. Its price tells you how much of the second currency is needed to equal one unit of the first currency.

For example, EUR/USD compares the euro with the US dollar. If the exchange rate is 1.1000, one euro is quoted as being worth 1.10 dollars. If the rate changes to 1.1200, one euro is worth more dollars than before.

Currency pairs are written using internationally recognised currency codes. EUR means euro, USD means US dollar, GBP means British pound, and JPY means Japanese yen.

If you are new to the market, start with What Is Forex? and then read How Does Forex Trading Work? to understand how these quotes are used in a trade.

2. Base Currency vs. Quote Currency

Every standard currency pair has two parts. The order matters because it determines how the price should be read.

Term Meaning In EUR/USD
Base currency The first currency in the pair; the unit being priced. EUR — euro
Quote currency The second currency; the currency used to express the price. USD — US dollar
Exchange rate How many units of the quote currency equal one unit of the base currency. At 1.1000, one euro is quoted at 1.10 dollars.

A simple memory trick is: first currency = one unit being priced; second currency = the price currency.

In GBP/USD at 1.2700, one British pound is quoted at 1.27 US dollars. In USD/JPY at 150.00, one US dollar is quoted at 150 Japanese yen.

Important

The quote is not a promise that you can exchange money at exactly that rate. A trading platform may show separate bid and ask prices, and transaction costs can affect the price available to you.

3. How to Read a Forex Quote

Read a pair from left to right. Start with one unit of the base currency, then read the number as its price in the quote currency.

Pair and quote Read it as
EUR/USD = 1.1000 One euro is quoted at 1.10 US dollars.
GBP/USD = 1.2700 One British pound is quoted at 1.27 US dollars.
USD/JPY = 150.00 One US dollar is quoted at 150 Japanese yen.
AUD/USD = 0.6600 One Australian dollar is quoted at 0.66 US dollars.

Notice that the quote currency changes depending on the pair. In EUR/USD, the price is expressed in dollars. In USD/JPY, it is expressed in yen. Do not assume that every pair is priced in US dollars.

4. What Does a Rising or Falling Pair Mean?

A currency pair compares two currencies, so its price reflects their relative value. If the pair rises, the base currency has increased in value relative to the quote currency over that period. If it falls, the base currency has decreased in value relative to the quote currency.

Example: EUR/USD rises

Suppose EUR/USD moves from 1.1000 to 1.1200. One euro was quoted at 1.10 dollars and is now quoted at 1.12 dollars. The euro has strengthened relative to the dollar over this interval, or equivalently, the dollar has weakened relative to the euro.

Example: EUR/USD falls

Suppose EUR/USD moves from 1.1000 to 1.0800. One euro now buys fewer dollars than before. The euro has weakened relative to the dollar over this interval.

A rising pair does not mean both currencies are becoming stronger against every other currency. It only describes their movement relative to each other. To compare either currency with a third currency, you need additional exchange rates.

5. What Does Buying or Selling a Pair Mean?

In a conventional forex quote, buying a pair means buying the base currency and selling the quote currency. Selling a pair means selling the base currency and buying the quote currency.

  • Buy EUR/USD: you take a position that benefits from an increase in EUR/USD, before considering trading costs.
  • Sell EUR/USD: you take a position that benefits from a decrease in EUR/USD, before considering trading costs.

This is why the order of the currencies matters. If you expect the euro to strengthen against the dollar, EUR/USD rising is consistent with that view. If you expect the dollar to strengthen against the euro, EUR/USD falling is consistent with that view.

This describes the direction of a position, not a recommendation to open one. The price can move against your expectation, and the result also depends on position size, spread, fees and execution.

6. Major, Minor and Exotic Currency Pairs

Traders commonly group currency pairs by how widely they are traded and which currencies they contain. These labels are useful, but they do not guarantee a particular spread, level of liquidity or trading cost. Conditions vary by provider and market environment.

Major pairs

Major pairs generally include the US dollar and another widely traded currency. Examples include EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY and USD/CHF.

Minor or cross pairs

Minor pairs generally combine widely traded currencies without the US dollar. Examples include EUR/GBP, EUR/JPY and GBP/JPY.

Exotic pairs

Exotic pairs generally combine a major currency with a currency from a smaller or emerging market. The exact classification can differ between providers. These pairs may have wider spreads or less liquidity in some conditions, so check the actual trading costs before making assumptions.

Beginner tip

Learn to read a pair and understand its costs before deciding which pair to study. A familiar currency name does not automatically make a pair easy or low-risk to trade.

7. Practical Examples: Understand the Numbers

Example A: What does EUR/USD = 1.1000 mean?

It means one euro is quoted at 1.10 US dollars. If you were converting €100 at that illustrative rate, the arithmetic would be:

€100 × 1.10 USD per EUR = $110

This is a simple conversion calculation, not a promise that a bank, exchange service or trading platform will offer that exact rate. Fees and the rate available to you may change the final amount.

Example B: The price moves from 1.1000 to 1.1050

The quote has increased by 0.0050 dollars per euro. For many pairs quoted to four decimal places, a pip is 0.0001, so this movement is 50 pips. Some pairs, such as many JPY pairs, conventionally use a different decimal place for a pip.

A 50-pip movement alone does not tell you how much money a trader made or lost. You also need to know the position size, direction, entry and exit prices, and trading costs. Learn more about what a pip is in forex and read How Does Forex Trading Work? to understand how these concepts fit into a trade.

Example C: Why the order of currencies matters

Suppose EUR/USD is 1.1000 and USD/EUR is quoted separately. These are opposite ways of expressing the relationship between the same two currencies. Mathematically, the reciprocal of 1.1000 is approximately 0.9091, so one dollar corresponds to about 0.9091 euros at the reciprocal rate before costs and quote differences.

In live markets, the bid and ask prices mean that the executable rate in one direction is not necessarily the exact mathematical reciprocal of the executable rate in the other direction.

8. Common Beginner Mistakes

  • Reversing the currencies: EUR/USD at 1.1000 means one euro is quoted at 1.10 dollars, not one dollar at 1.10 euros.
  • Assuming a rising pair means both currencies are stronger: the quote only describes the relative value of the two currencies.
  • Thinking a price movement equals a fixed cash profit: money gained or lost depends on position size, direction and costs.
  • Ignoring the spread: the displayed buy and sell prices may differ, creating a cost when entering or exiting a position.
  • Assuming all pairs behave alike: liquidity, volatility, trading hours and costs can vary between instruments and market conditions.
  • Confusing a currency conversion with a leveraged trade: exchanging money and trading a leveraged product can have very different costs, obligations and risks.

9. Test Your Understanding

Try answering these questions before looking at the explanations. The goal is to check whether you can read a quote, not to predict the market.

Question 1

EUR/USD is quoted at 1.2000. How many US dollars are quoted for one euro?

Show answer

$1.20. EUR is the base currency and USD is the quote currency.

Question 2

USD/JPY rises from 150.00 to 151.00. Which currency has strengthened relative to the other over this interval?

Show answer

The US dollar has strengthened relative to the Japanese yen in this quote, because one dollar now corresponds to more yen.

Question 3

If you buy EUR/USD, which currency are you buying in the pair's conventional trading interpretation?

Show answer

You are buying the base currency, EUR, and selling the quote currency, USD. This describes the position's direction, not its eventual result.

Question 4

EUR/USD moves up by 20 pips. Can you calculate the trader's cash profit from that information alone?

Show answer

No. You also need the position size, whether the trader bought or sold, the entry and exit prices, and relevant costs.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the simplest definition of a currency pair?

A currency pair compares one currency with another. Its quote tells you how much of the second currency is needed to equal one unit of the first currency.

What are the base and quote currencies?

The base currency is the first currency in the pair. The quote currency is the second. In GBP/USD, GBP is the base and USD is the quote.

Why do forex traders use currency pairs?

Exchange rates express the relative value of two currencies. Trading a pair allows a trader to take a position on how that relationship may change, while accepting the risk of an adverse move.

Is EUR/USD always the cheapest pair to trade?

No pair is guaranteed to have the lowest cost at all times. Spreads, commissions and execution conditions depend on the provider, account type, market conditions and time of day. Compare actual costs rather than relying on a general label.

Does buying a currency pair mean I receive the physical currency?

Not necessarily. Retail forex products can be structured differently. Some transactions involve currency exchange, while many retail trading products provide exposure to price movements without delivery of the underlying currencies. Check the product and provider's terms.

Can I predict a currency pair just by understanding its quote?

No. Understanding a quote tells you how to read the price, not where it will move next. Exchange rates can respond to interest rates, economic data, market expectations and unexpected events. No definition or simple calculation can guarantee a forecast.
