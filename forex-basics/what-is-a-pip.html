What Is a Pip in Forex?

By Ali Moayyed and Nasrin Kheyrdost

Learn how forex pips measure price movements, how to calculate pip changes in common currency pairs, and why pip value and position size matter when managing trading risk.

Understanding Pips in the Forex Market

A pip is a standard unit used to describe changes in a currency pair's exchange rate. Traders use pips to measure price movements, compare market changes, and discuss spreads and trade outcomes.

When EUR/USD moves from 1.1000 to 1.1010, the exchange rate has moved by 10 pips under the standard convention. However, the number of pips alone does not tell you how much money you gained or lost.

The monetary result also depends on position size, the currency pair, your account currency, and applicable trading costs.

What Does Pip Mean?

For most commonly traded non-JPY currency pairs, one pip represents a change of 0.0001 in the exchange rate. For many pairs involving the Japanese yen (JPY), one pip represents a change of 0.01.

Many trading platforms display an additional decimal digit, often called a fractional pip or pipette. This extra digit allows prices to be quoted more precisely, but it does not change the standard pip convention.

How to Calculate Pips

To calculate a price movement in pips, find the difference between the ending and starting prices, then divide that difference by the standard pip size for the currency pair. The direction of the movement tells you whether price rose or fell.

Example 1: EUR/USD

Suppose EUR/USD rises from 1.1000 to 1.1025. The difference is 0.0025, which equals 25 pips.

Calculation: 0.0025 ÷ 0.0001 = 25 pips up.

Example 2: GBP/USD

Suppose GBP/USD falls from 1.2750 to 1.2720. The difference is 0.0030, equivalent to 30 pips downward.

Calculation: 0.0030 ÷ 0.0001 = 30 pips down.

Example 3: USD/JPY

Suppose USD/JPY rises from 150.20 to 150.45. The difference is 0.25, which equals 25 pips under the standard JPY-pair convention.

Calculation: 0.25 ÷ 0.01 = 25 pips up.

Pips and Fractional Pips

A broker may quote EUR/USD as 1.10000 rather than 1.1000. In this five-decimal quote, the fifth decimal place is usually a fractional pip, equal to one-tenth of a standard pip.

For example, a movement from 1.10000 to 1.10010 is one standard pip, even though the final displayed digit changes by one.

For many JPY pairs, the third decimal place may represent a fractional pip. Always check the instrument specifications provided by your broker, particularly for less common instruments.

Quick Reference: Pip Size

EUR/USD 1.1000 → 1.1010 10 pips up
GBP/USD 1.2750 → 1.2720 30 pips down
USD/JPY 150.20 → 150.45 25 pips up

How Much Is One Pip Worth?

Pip size and pip value are different concepts. Pip size describes the price increment. Pip value describes the monetary effect of that movement for a particular position.

Pip value depends on the currency pair, trade size, and the currency in which your account is denominated. For pairs where USD is the quote currency, a standard lot of 100,000 units often has a pip value of approximately $10 per pip. This is a common illustration, not a universal value for every pair or account.

For example, if a position has a pip value of $1 and the market moves 20 pips in your favor, the gross result would be $20 before spreads, commissions, swaps, and other applicable costs. A 20-pip move against the position would produce a $20 gross loss under the same assumptions.

Do not estimate financial risk from pips alone. Verify the instrument's contract specifications and pip value using your provider's calculator or trading platform.

Why Pips Matter in Forex Trading

  • Measuring price movement: Pips help describe how far a currency pair has moved.
  • Understanding spreads: The difference between bid and ask prices is often expressed in pips or fractional pips.
  • Planning stop-loss distances: Traders may describe a stop-loss as a certain number of pips from an entry price, although gaps and slippage can affect the actual execution price.
  • Comparing trade outcomes: Pip results help compare price movements, but they do not replace monetary profit-and-loss calculations.

Common Beginner Mistakes

  • Confusing pips with money: A 20-pip move does not have the same monetary value for every position size or currency pair.
  • Counting the wrong decimal places: Most non-JPY pairs use the fourth decimal place for a standard pip, while JPY pairs commonly use the second.
  • Confusing pips with fractional pips: An extra displayed digit often represents one-tenth of a pip.
  • Ignoring trading costs: Gross pip movement does not include every spread, commission, financing charge, or execution effect.
  • Assuming a stop-loss guarantees the exact exit: In fast markets or price gaps, execution may differ from the requested stop price.

Practice: Count the Pips

Try calculating these examples before checking the answers. Use the standard pip conventions described above.

  1. EUR/USD moves from 1.0850 to 1.0870. How many pips has it risen?
  2. GBP/USD moves from 1.2600 to 1.2575. How many pips has it fallen?
  3. USD/JPY moves from 149.80 to 150.10. How many pips has it risen?
Show the answers

1. 20 pips up: (1.0870 − 1.0850) ÷ 0.0001 = 20.

2. 25 pips down: (1.2600 − 1.2575) ÷ 0.0001 = 25.

3. 30 pips up: (150.10 − 149.80) ÷ 0.01 = 30.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a pip always the fourth decimal place?

No. Most non-JPY currency pairs use the fourth decimal place, while JPY pairs commonly use the second decimal place for a standard pip. Check the provider's specifications for other instruments.

What is the difference between a pip and a pipette?

A pipette is commonly one-tenth of a standard pip. It is often shown as the fifth decimal place for non-JPY pairs or the third decimal place for JPY pairs.

Does a higher pip count mean a larger profit?

Not necessarily. Monetary results also depend on position size, pip value, account currency, and trading costs. A larger pip movement can also mean a larger loss.

Can I use pips to measure risk?

Pips can describe the distance between an entry and a stop-loss, but estimating financial risk also requires position size, pip value, execution conditions, and applicable costs.
