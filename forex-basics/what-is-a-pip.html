FOREX BASICS

What Is a Pip in Forex?

By Ali Moayyed and Nasrin Kheyrdost

Learn how pips measure forex price movements, how to calculate pip changes in major currency pairs, and why pip value matters when evaluating trading costs and risk.

Understanding Pips in Forex

A pip is a standard unit used to describe changes in a currency pair's exchange rate. Traders use pips to measure price movements, compare changes, and discuss spreads and trade outcomes.

For most non-JPY currency pairs, one pip is 0.0001. For many pairs involving the Japanese yen (JPY), one pip is 0.01. These conventions help traders describe price movements consistently.

Remember: The number of pips alone does not tell you how much money you have gained or lost. The financial result also depends on position size, the instrument, account currency, and trading costs.

What Does Pip Mean?

A pip is a conventional unit for measuring the change in an exchange rate. The standard pip size depends on the currency pair and, for some instruments, the provider's contract specifications.

Pair type Example price Typical pip size
EUR/USD 1.1000 0.0001
GBP/USD 1.2750 0.0001
USD/JPY 150.20 0.01

Many platforms display an additional decimal digit. This is commonly called a fractional pip or pipette. It gives a more precise quote without changing the standard pip size.

How to Calculate Pips

Subtract the starting price from the ending price, then divide the absolute difference by the standard pip size for that pair. Keep the direction separate so you can identify whether price moved up or down.

Pip movement = Price difference ÷ Pip size

For most non-JPY pairs, pip size = 0.0001.

For many JPY pairs, pip size = 0.01.

Example 1: EUR/USD rises

The price moves from 1.1000 to 1.1025.

Difference: 1.1025 − 1.1000 = 0.0025.

Calculation: 0.0025 ÷ 0.0001 = 25 pips up.

Example 2: GBP/USD falls

The price moves from 1.2750 to 1.2720.

Difference: 1.2750 − 1.2720 = 0.0030.

Calculation: 0.0030 ÷ 0.0001 = 30 pips down.

Example 3: USD/JPY rises

The price moves from 150.20 to 150.45.

Difference: 150.45 − 150.20 = 0.25.

Calculation: 0.25 ÷ 0.01 = 25 pips up.

Pips and Fractional Pips

Some platforms quote EUR/USD to five decimal places, such as 1.10000. In a typical five-decimal quote, the fifth decimal digit represents a fractional pip, equal to one-tenth of a standard pip.

EUR/USD quote change Movement
1.10000 → 1.10010 1 pip
1.10000 → 1.10001 0.1 pip (1 pipette)
1.10000 → 1.10100 10 pips

For many JPY pairs, a three-decimal quote may show fractional pips in the third decimal place. Check the instrument specifications supplied by your broker, especially for less common instruments.

How Much Is One Pip Worth?

Pip size and pip value are different. Pip size describes the price increment. Pip value describes the monetary effect of that movement for a particular position.

Pip value depends on the currency pair, trade size, exchange rate, and account currency. For a standard 100,000-unit position in many USD-quoted major pairs, one pip is approximately USD 10 when the account currency is USD. This is an illustration, not a universal rule.

A simple monetary example

Suppose the pip value of a position is USD 1. A favorable movement of 20 pips would produce a gross gain of USD 20 before costs.

A 20-pip move against the position would produce a gross loss of USD 20 under the same assumptions.

If your account uses a different currency, the pip value may need to be converted. Verify the contract size, position units, and pip value using your trading platform or provider's calculator.

How Pips Relate to Spreads and Trading Costs

The spread is the difference between the bid and ask prices. It is often expressed in pips or fractional pips. For example, if a pair has an ask price of 1.1002 and a bid price of 1.1000, the spread is 0.0002, or 2 pips under the usual EUR/USD convention.

The spread is only one possible trading cost. Depending on the account and instrument, commissions, overnight financing, slippage, and other charges may also affect the final result.

Why Pips Matter for Risk Management

  • Measuring movement: Pips provide a common way to describe how far price has moved.
  • Planning stop-loss distances: Traders may express the distance between entry and stop-loss in pips.
  • Estimating financial exposure: Pip distance must be considered alongside position size and pip value to estimate a potential loss.
  • Comparing trades: Pip results can help compare price movements, but monetary outcomes also depend on trade size and costs.
Risk warning

A stop-loss order does not guarantee execution at the exact requested price. Gaps, fast markets, and slippage can cause a different fill price. Leveraged forex trading can result in substantial losses.

Common Beginner Mistakes

  • Confusing pips with money: A 20-pip move does not have the same monetary value for every position.
  • Counting the wrong decimal places: Most non-JPY pairs use 0.0001 as a standard pip, while many JPY pairs use 0.01.
  • Confusing pips with pipettes: An additional displayed digit is often one-tenth of a standard pip.
  • Ignoring costs: Gross price movement does not account for all spreads, commissions, or financing charges.
  • Assuming a stop price is guaranteed: Execution can differ from the requested price during gaps or volatile conditions.

Practice: Count the Pips

Try each example before opening the answer section. Use the standard pip conventions explained above.

  1. EUR/USD moves from 1.0850 to 1.0870. How many pips has it risen?
  2. GBP/USD moves from 1.2600 to 1.2575. How many pips has it fallen?
  3. USD/JPY moves from 149.80 to 150.10. How many pips has it risen?
Show the answers and calculations

1. 20 pips up. (1.0870 − 1.0850) ÷ 0.0001 = 20.

2. 25 pips down. (1.2600 − 1.2575) ÷ 0.0001 = 25.

3. 30 pips up. (150.10 − 149.80) ÷ 0.01 = 30.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a pip always the fourth decimal place?

No. Most non-JPY currency pairs use 0.0001 as the standard pip size, while many JPY pairs use 0.01. Check the specifications for the instrument you trade.

What is the difference between a pip and a pipette?

A pipette is commonly one-tenth of a standard pip. It often appears as the fifth decimal place for non-JPY pairs or the third for JPY pairs.

Does a higher pip count mean a larger profit?

Not necessarily. Profit or loss also depends on position size, pip value, account currency, direction of the trade, and trading costs.

Can I use pips to measure risk?

Pips can describe the distance between an entry and a stop-loss. Estimating monetary risk also requires position size, pip value, and consideration of execution conditions and costs.
