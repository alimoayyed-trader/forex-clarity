How Does Forex Trading Work?

Forex trading involves exchanging one currency for another. Learn how currency pairs, buy and sell orders, price movements, trading costs, leverage and risk fit together in a typical trade.

Forex is short for foreign exchange. In the foreign exchange market, currencies are quoted in pairs such as EUR/USD or GBP/JPY. A trader may open a position based on the expectation that one currency will strengthen or weaken relative to the other.

A trade's result depends on the direction and size of the price movement, the position size, the entry and exit prices, and the costs involved. A trade can lose money even when a trader understands how the market works.

Risk warning: Forex trading can lead to substantial losses. Leverage magnifies exposure, and losses can occur quickly. This article is for educational purposes only and is not financial advice or a recommendation to trade.

1. Understand currency pairs

A forex quote contains two currencies. In EUR/USD, EUR is the base currency and USD is the quote currency. The quoted price tells you how many US dollars correspond to one euro.

If EUR/USD is 1.0800, one euro corresponds to 1.08 US dollars at that quoted rate. If the price rises to 1.0850, the euro has strengthened against the dollar over that interval. If the price falls to 1.0750, the euro has weakened against the dollar.

Currency prices are affected by many factors, including interest-rate expectations, economic data, central-bank decisions, political developments and market sentiment. Price movements are uncertain.

2. Choose whether to buy or sell

A trader generally chooses a direction based on how they expect the quoted pair to move.

  • Buy (go long): you expect the pair's price to rise. Buying EUR/USD means taking a position that benefits from the euro strengthening relative to the dollar, before costs.
  • Sell (go short): you expect the pair's price to fall. Selling EUR/USD means taking a position that benefits from the euro weakening relative to the dollar, before costs.

Being correct about direction is not enough by itself. Position size, execution price, spreads, commissions and other charges can all affect the result.

3. A simple forex trade example

Imagine a trader buys EUR/USD at an illustrative entry price of 1.0800 and later closes the position at 1.0850. The quoted price has moved upward by 0.0050.

Price movement = 1.0850 − 1.0800 = 0.0050

For most major currency pairs quoted to four decimal places, one pip is typically 0.0001. This price movement is therefore 50 pips, before accounting for the spread and other costs.

Item Illustrative value
Entry price 1.0800
Exit price 1.0850
Price movement +0.0050, or 50 pips
Position size Not specified
Net profit or loss Cannot be calculated from price movement alone

The actual financial result depends on the position size, execution prices, spread, commission and any applicable financing charges. A similar move in the opposite direction would have the opposite effect on a buy position.

For more detail, read What Is a Pip in Forex?

Remember: A pip describes a price movement. It does not represent the same monetary amount for every position. Position size and the currency pair affect pip value.

4. Understand trading costs

Trading costs reduce the result of a trade. The exact charges depend on the instrument, account type and provider.

Spread

The spread is the difference between the bid and ask prices. The bid is the price at which you can sell, while the ask is the price at which you can buy. A wider spread generally means a higher immediate trading cost, all else being equal.

Commission

Some account types charge a separate commission when a position is opened, closed or both. Compare the total costs rather than assuming an account with no separate commission is always cheaper.

Overnight financing

Positions held overnight may incur financing charges or credits, sometimes called swaps. The amount and conditions depend on the instrument, the provider and the direction of the position.

Slippage

Slippage occurs when an order is executed at a different price from the one expected. It can happen during volatile markets, around important news or when available liquidity changes. A stop-loss order does not always guarantee execution at the exact stop price.

5. Understand leverage and margin

Leverage allows a trader to control a position whose market exposure is larger than the funds required as margin. Margin is the collateral required to open and maintain a leveraged position.

For example, if a hypothetical position has a market exposure of $10,000 and the applicable margin requirement is 5%, the required initial margin would be $500. This is an illustration only; actual requirements depend on the product, provider and applicable regulations.

The margin is not the maximum possible loss. If the market moves against the position, losses can accumulate quickly. Depending on the provider's rules, positions may be closed if the account no longer meets margin requirements.

To understand leverage ratios, margin requirements, effective leverage and the risks in more detail, read our complete guide: What Is Leverage in Forex?

Important: Higher leverage increases market exposure relative to the funds committed. It does not improve the probability of a trade being correct and can magnify losses.

6. Learn how orders work

Trading platforms offer different order types. Their names are similar across many providers, but the exact execution rules can vary.

  • Market order: requests execution at the best available price. The final execution price may differ from the displayed quote.
  • Limit order: seeks execution at a specified price or better. Execution is not guaranteed if the market does not meet the order conditions.
  • Stop order: becomes active when its trigger condition is reached. In fast markets, execution may occur at a different price.
  • Stop-loss: is intended to close a position at a defined risk level. It helps structure a trade but cannot eliminate every risk.
  • Take-profit: is intended to close a position at a chosen target, subject to execution conditions.

Before opening a position, define the reason for entry, the condition that would invalidate the idea, the planned exit and the amount you could lose if the trade fails.

7. Manage risk before trading

Risk management does not guarantee a profit. Its purpose is to make potential losses explicit and help avoid positions whose risks are unacceptable.

  • Never risk money needed for essential expenses or use borrowed money simply to fund a trade.
  • Determine position size from your planned stop distance and the amount you are willing to risk.
  • Account for spreads, commissions, slippage and financing charges when evaluating a trade.
  • Understand that actual losses can exceed the amount planned for some orders or market conditions.
  • Avoid increasing position size simply to recover a previous loss.
  • Practise in a demo environment and keep a trading journal before considering real-money trading.

8. Beginner's learning checklist

Before considering a live trade, make sure you can explain each of the following in your own words:

  1. How a currency pair is quoted and what its price means.
  2. The difference between bid, ask, spread and pip.
  3. How buy and sell positions respond to price movements.
  4. How position size affects potential gains and losses.
  5. How leverage and margin work and why they are risky.
  6. How orders work and why execution prices can differ.
  7. How to define a risk limit before entering a trade.

Frequently asked questions

Can beginners make money trading forex?

Forex outcomes are uncertain, and many retail traders lose money. Results depend on market conditions, trading costs, position sizing, experience and risk management. No method can guarantee profits.

How much money do I need to start?

Minimum deposits and trading conditions vary by provider and jurisdiction. A low minimum deposit does not make trading low-risk. Understand the product, costs and possible losses before depositing money.

Is forex trading open 24 hours a day?

The global forex market operates across financial centres during the business week. Exact trading hours, breaks and holiday schedules depend on the instrument and provider. Check the schedule on your platform.

Is leverage necessary to trade forex?

Leverage is available through many retail providers, but it is not a shortcut to profit. It increases exposure and can magnify losses. Availability, limits and protections vary by jurisdiction and provider.

Can a stop-loss eliminate trading risk?

No. A stop-loss can help define an exit level, but execution may differ from the requested price in fast or gapping markets. It cannot guarantee that every loss will remain within the planned amount.
